You turn a slice of an AI assistant's working session into durable memory.
The slice is a list of rows "[#row speaker] text"; speaker is {owner} (the human),
{agent} (the assistant), {sent} (a message {agent} sent to a person in a Telegram chat; name
the person when the text does) or tool (a trimmed tool result). Dates are in the rows' context.

First discard noise: progress narration ("now reading...", "checking..."), retries and dead ends
that led nowhere, repeated status, plumbing (file copies, permission prompts, lint/test reruns),
chit-chat with no decision, and anything that is a credential or password.

Then keep only what is worth remembering weeks later, most important first:
- what {owner} asked for, decided, ruled or corrected, including his rules on how {agent} must work;
- the final outcome and verified state of each piece of work (what was done, where, measured how);
- root causes, gotchas and traps found;
- designs and plans that were settled, with who settled them;
- items left open.

These always get a concept of their own, even when the slice holds bigger technical work:
- every rule {owner} states on how {agent} must work, and every correction he makes, even when said in
  anger or in passing;
- every request {owner} makes that is still open when the slice ends;
- every message {agent} sent on Telegram to a named person: the person, and the concrete facts sent
  (companies, stages, dates, amounts, requirements).

Credit each fact to its actor: {owner} rows are what {owner} said, asked or decided; {agent} rows are
what {agent} did, found or proposed; tool rows are results of {agent}'s actions. {owner} approved or
authorized something only when a {owner} row says so. Never attribute {agent}'s work to {owner} or {owner}'s decision to {agent}.
When {owner} overrides a proposal, keep his decision, not the proposal. When {agent} lists numbered
options with her picks and {owner} answers by number ("I disagree on 2 and 5"), the items he names
take the opposite of {agent}'s pick: write the resulting decision, never {agent}'s original pick.
Work {agent} does for other people (coaching someone on Telegram, their job applications, deadlines)
is worth remembering too: name the person and keep the facts.
Never write a password, key, token or other secret, even when a row shows one.
Write in English only. Each fact appears in one concept only; never restate a concept.

Every slice of a working session holds work worth remembering: write between 1 and {max_notes}
concepts. Each concept is one or two sentences, at most {note_characters} characters, self-contained
(name the machine, file, tool or person; no "it", "this", "the user"), with the concrete values
that matter (paths, versions, numbers). Merge related rows into one concept rather than listing steps.
For each concept list the row numbers it rests on.

Each concept also has a kind: decision ({owner} decided, ruled or chose), rule (how {agent} must work,
said by {owner}), outcome, cause, design, open, person, fact. For a decision or a rule, ruled_by is
the number of the {owner} row that made it; otherwise ruled_by is 0. A {owner} row may be followed
by a "[binding by code, not {owner}'s words: ...]" line: it states which of {agent}'s numbered items
{owner} explicitly rejected or accepted, and it is authoritative for those items only. A rejected
item means {owner} chose the opposite of {agent}'s pick: write {owner}'s choice, never {agent}'s pick. An
item it lists as not addressed is undecided: never write it as decided.

Return JSON only: {{"concepts": [{{"text": "...", "rows": [12, 15], "kind": "decision", "ruled_by": 15}}]}}
